## About

I am a doctoral student in Computer Science at [Örebro University]
(https://www.oru.se/english/employee/jonas_goncalves), Sweden, in the School of
Science and Technology. My research lies at the intersection of probabilistic
machine learning, neuro-symbolic AI, probabilistic circuits, and knowledge
representation.

I study how to make machine-learning and probabilistic models expressive while
preserving enough mathematical structure for useful reasoning and inference to
remain tractable. A central focus is probabilistic circuits and related
tractable representations: their expressive power, computational properties,
learning algorithms, and efficient implementations.

## Research interests

- **Probabilistic circuits and tractable inference**: circuit structure,
expressivity, learning, inference, and computational complexity.
- **Neuro-symbolic reasoning**: combining connectionist components with
symbolic representations in order to reliably reason under uncertainty.
- **Knowledge compilation**: translate logical constraints into representations
amenable for exact probabilistic inference and other queries that woudln't be
tractable otherwise.
- **Extensions of circuit models**: current work exploring algebraic,
non-monotonic, complex-valued, and operator-valued representations beyond
conventional non-negative and scalar valued probabilistic models. 
- **Research software**: clear, mathematically faithful implementations for
numerical and differentiable computing, with an interest in JAX, Rust,
functional programming and GPU-friendly algorithms.

## Selected projects

### [bSharp](https://github.com/jonasrlg/bSharp)

A Rust research library for bottom-up knowledge compilation with structured
`AND-SUM` circuits. It provides circuit manipulation, Boolean operations,
weighted model counting, and compilers for CNF, DNF, and probabilistic-logic
inputs; it is a vehicle for exploring circuit representations that use signed
structure.

### [PASDD](https://github.com/jonasrlg/PASDD)

Research code and an experimental pipeline for compiling probabilistic
answer-set programs into decision diagrams, including SDDs and OBDDs. The Python
 codebase includes program transformations, ordering heuristics, compilation
experiments, and analyses of resulting circuit sizes and runtimes.

### [dPASP](https://github.com/kamel-usp/dpasp)

A collaborative differentiable probabilistic answer-set programming framework
for specifying uncertain logic programs and combining them with learned
components. I was involved in its development and research; the project provides
a concrete link between my MSc work in neuro-symbolic reasoning, probabilistic
logic programming, and knowledge compilation.
